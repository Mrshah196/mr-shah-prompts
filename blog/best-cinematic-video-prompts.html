Best Cinematic Video Prompts for AI Video Generators

What makes an AI video look like a movie scene instead of a random clip? It is almost never the tool — it is the prompt. Here is the cinematic formula, and ready-to-copy prompts for Veo, Runway, Kling and Sora.

The cinematic formula

A cinematic prompt has five ingredients. Miss one and the video still works — but include all five and it looks like film:

1. Subject + action: who is doing what? ("A lone traveller walking through a desert")

2. Camera movement: "slow dolly shot", "aerial drone shot", "tracking shot", "orbiting camera". Movement is what separates video from a still image.

3. Lighting: "golden hour", "moody fog", "neon night glow", "soft morning light". Light sets the emotion.

4. Lens and look: "35mm film", "anamorphic lens", "shallow depth of field", "film grain". These words tell the model you want cinema, not a phone video.

5. Mood and atmosphere: "epic", "mysterious", "dreamy", "tense". One mood word unifies the whole scene.

Example cinematic prompts

Desert journey: "Cinematic aerial drone shot of a lone traveller walking across golden sand dunes at sunset, long shadows, warm orange light, epic orchestral mood, 35mm film look, slow smooth camera movement."
Rainy city: "Tracking shot through a rainy neon-lit Tokyo street at night, reflections on wet pavement, a man with an umbrella walks away from camera, moody cinematic lighting, anamorphic lens, film grain."
Mountain dawn: "Slow dolly shot revealing misty mountain peaks at dawn, first light breaking through clouds, peaceful and majestic mood, cinematic wide shot, soft golden light."

Notice the pattern: subject, camera, light, lens, mood — in that order. Once you learn the pattern, you can write your own in seconds.

Which tools work best?

Google Veo — excellent for realistic motion and longer scenes. Runway — great control over camera and style. Kling — strong for dramatic, high-detail clips. Sora — impressive for complex, story-like scenes. The same prompt works in all of them; small tweaks to style words are sometimes needed.

Tips for better results

One scene per prompt. Don't ask for a whole story in one clip — generate scenes separately and edit them together.

Keep motion slow. Words like "slow", "smooth" and "gentle" prevent the shaky, warped motion AI sometimes produces.

Try, then adjust. Change one ingredient at a time — first the camera, then the light — until the scene feels right.
