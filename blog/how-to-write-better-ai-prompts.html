How to Write Better AI Prompts: A Beginner's Guide

Most AI images fail because of the prompt, not the tool. Here are 5 simple rules that turn a vague idea into a stunning result — whether you use ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney or any other AI generator.

1. Be specific, not vague

AI tools cannot read your mind. "A beautiful man" gives you a random result. "A young man in a black leather jacket, standing on a rainy street in Tokyo at night, neon signs reflecting on wet pavement" gives you a scene. Every detail you add is a decision you make instead of leaving it to chance.

2. Name the style

Style changes everything. Add words like "cinematic", "photorealistic", "oil painting", "anime style", "3D render" or "vintage film photo". Without a style word, the AI guesses — and usually guesses the most generic option.

3. Describe the light

Lighting is the difference between flat and dramatic. Try "golden hour sunlight", "soft studio light", "moody low-key lighting" or "neon city glow". Photographers obsess over light for a reason — and so should your prompts.

4. Add camera and angle

For images and especially videos, mention the camera: "close-up portrait", "wide aerial shot", "low angle", "85mm lens, shallow depth of field". This one line often makes a prompt look professional instead of amateur.

5. Set the mood

Words like "peaceful", "epic", "mysterious", "joyful" or "melancholic" guide the whole feeling of the output. Mood is the shortcut to emotion — and emotion is what makes people stop and look.

Bad prompt vs good prompt

✗ Bad: "A girl in a garden."
✓ Good: "A young woman in a white summer dress standing in a blooming rose garden at golden hour, soft warm sunlight, cinematic photography, shallow depth of field, joyful and dreamy mood."

Same idea — completely different result. The second prompt decides the subject, the light, the style and the mood. The first leaves all of it to luck.

Common mistakes to avoid

Too many ideas at once. One scene per prompt works best. If you want five things, make five prompts.

Contradictory words. "Dark bright room" confuses the model. Pick one direction.

Copying blindly. A prompt that worked for someone else's tool may need small tweaks for yours. Adjust the style words and try again.
